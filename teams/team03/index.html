Amazon Product Recommendation System

Discover related products through user interaction patterns. Our project explores collaborative recommendation techniques using product co-occurrence, similarity measures, and ensemble ranking to identify products that users may also find relevant.

Problem Statement

E-commerce platforms offer a large number of products, making it difficult for customers to discover relevant items among the available choices. Recommendation systems address this challenge by identifying relationships between products and helping users explore items that may match their interests.

The objective of this project is to develop a product-to-product recommendation approach using Amazon product reviews. By analyzing user–product interactions, we identify products that share common users, calculate similarity scores, and rank related products to generate recommendations.

The central question is: Can patterns in user–product interactions help us identify and rank related products?

Dataset

We use the Amazon Baby product review dataset from the Stanford Network Analysis Project (SNAP). The dataset contains customer reviews and product identifiers that allow us to study relationships between products based on shared users.

The important attributes include:

  • User ID: Identifies the customer who submitted a review.
  • Product ID: Identifies the reviewed product.
  • Rating: Records the customer's product rating.
  • Review text and summary: Provide textual feedback when available.
  • Review time: Records when the review was submitted, when available.

We focus primarily on user IDs and product IDs for interaction-based recommendation. Ratings and other review attributes are used for understanding the dataset and conducting exploratory analysis.

Approach

Our recommendation pipeline converts customer activity into product-to-product relationships. It uses multiple similarity measures and combines them into an ensemble score to rank candidate products.

  • Data preprocessing: Load the Amazon review data, standardize column names, validate required fields, and remove records with missing essential values.
  • Exploratory data analysis: Examine rating distributions, user activity, product popularity, and the overall structure of customer interactions.
  • User–product interaction matrix: Represent each unique user–product interaction in a sparse matrix to efficiently process the dataset.
  • Product co-occurrence: Calculate how many users interacted with each pair of products using the product co-occurrence matrix.
  • Similarity calculation: Calculate cosine similarity and Jaccard similarity for eligible product pairs, while also incorporating a normalized support score.
  • Ensemble ranking: Combine the similarity components into a weighted score and rank product pairs to identify the strongest relationships.
  • Recommendation and visualization: Retrieve the highest-ranked related products for a selected product and visualize product relationships using a network graph.

Recommendation Methodology

The system combines three complementary signals: cosine similarity, Jaccard similarity, and normalized shared-user support. The ensemble score is calculated as:

Ensemble Score = 0.45 × Cosine Similarity + 0.30 × Jaccard Similarity + 0.25 × Support Score

Cosine similarity measures shared-user overlap relative to the interaction counts of the two products. Jaccard similarity measures the proportion of shared users among all users associated with either product. The support component incorporates the number of shared users, using logarithmic normalization.

By combining these signals, the system considers both the strength of the relationship and the amount of interaction evidence supporting it. Product pairs with insufficient shared-user support are excluded before ranking.

Tools and Technologies

  • Python: Core implementation and data processing.
  • Pandas and NumPy: Data cleaning, tabular analysis, and numerical computation.
  • SciPy: Sparse matrices and efficient co-occurrence calculations.
  • Matplotlib and Seaborn: Exploratory data analysis and result visualization.
  • NetworkX: Construction and visualization of product relationship networks.
  • Jupyter Notebook: Experimentation, analysis, and execution of the recommendation pipeline.

Expected Outcome

The project aims to identify meaningful relationships among products using customer interaction patterns. Its outputs include product-pair similarity scores, ranked related products, descriptive statistics, and network visualizations that help explain how products are connected.

The approach provides an interpretable foundation for product recommendations. Its effectiveness depends on the available interaction data, the reliability of shared-user evidence, and subsequent evaluation of recommendation quality.
